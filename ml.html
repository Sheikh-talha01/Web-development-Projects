Naive Bayes Solution (Algebraic Method)

1. The Goal

We want to find the class \(y\) that makes the following most likely, given \(x_1=1\) and \(x_2=0\):

\[\text{Probability} \propto P(Y=y) \cdot P(x_1=1|Y=y) \cdot P(x_2=0|Y=y)\]

2. Plug in the Numbers

  • \(P(Y=y) = \frac{1}{32}\)
  • \(P(x_1=1|Y=y) = \frac{y}{46}\)
  • \(P(x_2=0|Y=y) = 1 - \frac{y}{62}\)

Combining these (and ignoring the constants \(\frac{1}{32}\) and \(\frac{1}{46}\)):

\[\text{Score} = y \cdot \left(1 - \frac{y}{62}\right) = y \cdot \left(\frac{62 - y}{62}\right)\]

3. Simplified Expression

Since we just want to find the best \(y\), we can ignore the \(\frac{1}{62}\) as well. We just need to maximize:

\[g(y) = y \cdot (62 - y)\]

4. The "Middle Point" Logic (No Calculus)

The expression \(y \cdot (62 - y)\) represents a parabola that opens downwards.

  • It hits zero at \(y = 0\).
  • It hits zero at \(y = 62\).

Because parabolas are perfectly symmetric, the highest peak (maximum) must be exactly in the middle of these two zeros:

\[\text{Peak} = \frac{0 + 62}{2} = 31\]
Conclusion: The most likely class is 31.
